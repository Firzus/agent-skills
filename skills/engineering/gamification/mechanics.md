# Mechanics catalog

Per-mechanic evidence, use conditions, and failure modes. Evidence tags:
**[Empirical]** peer-reviewed study or meta-analysis; **[Industry]** reported
by practitioners at scale, not peer-reviewed.

## Points / XP

- **Evidence** [Empirical]: Mekler et al. 2017 — points/levels/leaderboards
  increased performance *quantity* but did **not** raise intrinsic motivation
  or competence satisfaction. They are extrinsic performance incentives, not
  motivation machines.
- **Use when**: you need a lightweight, granular feedback signal tied to
  behaviors you actually value.
- **Backfires when**: points reward volume over quality (users optimize the
  metric — Goodhart's law) or stand in for meaning (pointsification).

## Badges / achievements

- **Evidence** [Empirical]: Hamari 2017, two-year field experiment (n≈3,000)
  on a trading service — the badge condition showed significantly more
  posting, transactions, and activity.
- **Use when**: badges mark meaningful milestones or steer users toward
  underused features. Unexpected badges dodge the overjustification trap.
- **Backfires when**: trivial ("you logged in!"), infinite, or the goal
  itself; reads as condescension to expert audiences.

## Leaderboards

- **Evidence** [Empirical, heterogeneous]: performance gains (Mekler 2017),
  but repeated findings of demotivation for low-ranked users; trait
  competitiveness moderates who benefits.
- **Use when**: the audience is opt-in competitive. Make boards
  **relative/local**: leagues and brackets (Duolingo-style), rank among
  similar peers, or self-referential ("you vs your last week").
- **Backfires when**: global, permanent, or mandatory; punishes newcomers,
  invites cheating and metric gaming, turns toxic in workplace settings.

## Streaks

- **Evidence**: loss aversion is robust [Empirical] (losses loom ~2× gains);
  streak specifics are [Industry] — Duolingo's experiment program reports
  streak freeze cutting churn ~21% for at-risk users, and users past a
  seven-day streak far likelier to return.
- **Use when**: the behavior genuinely benefits from daily consistency —
  and **always with forgiveness** (freeze, repair): forgiveness *increases*
  retention by lowering anxiety.
- **Backfires when**: it drives guilt, hollow streak-saving minimal actions
  instead of the real behavior, or a single break causes total abandonment.
  Streaks are a black-hat drive (loss & avoidance) — accent, not engine.

## Progress bars / levels

- **Evidence** [Empirical]: goal-gradient and endowed-progress effects
  (Kivetz et al. 2006) — effort accelerates near the goal; pre-seeded
  progress speeds completion; a post-reward reset (effort dip) is documented
  after goal completion.
- **Use when**: a definable finish line exists — onboarding checklists,
  profile completion, course progress. Chain the next goal immediately after
  each completion to absorb the post-reward dip.
- **Backfires when**: progress is fake (endowed progress shades into
  deception) or the bar stalls near the end, where frustration peaks.

## Variable rewards

- **Evidence** [Empirical]: variable-ratio reinforcement schedules produce
  the most persistent behavior (operant conditioning). Ethically the
  highest-risk mechanic.
- **Use when**: adding pleasant surprise — unexpected bonuses and varied
  content, which also avoid overjustification.
- **Backfires when**: tied to money or compulsion loops. Loot-box-style
  mechanics face active gambling-adjacent regulation in several EU states.

## Social / cooperative mechanics

- **Evidence**: relatedness is an SDT need [Empirical]; HEXAD data shows the
  most common user types (Philanthropist, Socialiser, Free Spirit) respond
  to meaning and connection, not competition.
- **Use when**: almost always — teams, gifting, mentoring are safer than
  competition because cooperation produces no losers.
- **Backfires when**: social exposure is forced, violating autonomy and
  privacy.

## Narrative, customization, creativity tools

- **Evidence**: aligned with autonomy and purpose (white-hat drives; RECIPE's
  choice/play/reflection) — [Framework] with indirect empirical support.
  Underused relative to PBL even though the field knows better (Koivisto &
  Hamari 2019).
- **Use when**: you want intrinsic engagement — these serve autonomy and
  meaning directly instead of routing through a point economy.
