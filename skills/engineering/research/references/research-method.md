# Gather, challenge, and synthesize evidence

## Research a bounded question

1. Prefer owning sources: official documentation for product behavior, original studies
   for empirical findings, code or reproducible observations for implementation.
   Secondary sources can identify leads or experiences; label their role.
2. Search through distinct relevant angles and read sources before citing. Snippets
   are leads, not evidence. Seek broad coverage and several independent origins where
   available; do not manufacture a quota when only one authoritative source exists.
3. Check dates, versions, populations, methods, and applicability. Distinguish a product
   contract, reported observation, author's preference, and comparative measurement.
4. For decision-driving claims, seek counterevidence and boundary cases. Corroborate
   disputed or high-impact findings independently; syndicated copies count as one origin.
5. Record precise supporting passages or faithful paraphrases, contrary evidence,
   qualifications, and unresolved leads using the dossier IDs.
6. Update coverage after each pass. Follow credible leads that can change the answer;
   mark access failures explicitly and vary the strategy before concluding no evidence
   is available. Respect supplied-source restrictions and explicit resource limits.

Keep private content out of public queries and services unless authorized. Store
minimal excerpts, not wholesale source copies or secrets. If browsing is unavailable,
do not present volatile external claims as verified current facts.

**Done:** assigned questions have supported answers or demonstrated gaps; relevant
counterevidence checked. Otherwise return incomplete coverage and the next useful lead.

## Verify without voting truth

Inspect each important claim against its original passage and relevant version.
Check whether the evidence establishes the exact wording, conditions, and magnitude,
not merely a related fact. Require reasons for verdicts:

| Verdict | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supported | Evidence supports the stated claim within its scope |
| Qualified | Supported only with stated conditions, exceptions, or narrower wording |
| Contradicted | Evidence actually conflicts under comparable conditions |
| Contested | Credible incompatible evidence remains unresolved |
| Unverified | Required source/access/check is unavailable, including tool or rate-limit failure |
| Inferred | Reasoned conclusion beyond what sources directly establish |

A caveat is not automatically a contradiction. An inaccessible page is not a
refutation. Agreement between agents cannot establish source independence.
Keep minority counterevidence and the reasons for rejection through synthesis.

Recommend corrected wording or further research. Do not silently delete inconvenient
claims, upgrade uncertainty, or fill missing evidence with confident prose.

**Done:** claims have evidence-based verdicts, limitations, and actionable remaining gaps.

## Synthesize checked results

- Answer the question first, with citations next to material claims.
- Preserve qualifications and disagreement. Remove unsupported assertions from the
  factual conclusion, but retain their disposition and reasons in the ledger.
- Separate facts, inferences, recommendations, and user choices. Compare relevant
  alternatives, including retaining the current approach.
- Report coverage and missing evidence; never equate source count, unanimous votes,
  valid JSON, or a successful worker exit with a complete answer.
- If evidence cannot settle a choice, propose a bounded trial: baseline, one changed
  variable, representative tasks, success/failure criteria, cost, and rollback.
  Label it unrun; obtain authorization before project or external-state changes.

**Done:** conclusions match the ledger and answer conditions; the reader can trace
what is known, disputed, and still needed without this conversation.
